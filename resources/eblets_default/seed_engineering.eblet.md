## Facts

**Q: What is the principle of superposition in structural engineering?**
A: The principle of superposition states that the net response (stress, displacement) in a linear system caused by multiple loads equals the sum of responses from each individual load applied separately.

**Q: What is Ohm's law in electrical engineering?**
A: Ohm's law V = IR states that voltage equals current times resistance. This fundamental relationship governs the behavior of resistive electrical circuits.

**Q: What is the Reynolds number?**
A: The Reynolds number (Re = ρvL/μ) is a dimensionless quantity in fluid mechanics that predicts whether flow will be laminar (low Re) or turbulent (high Re). It is the ratio of inertial to viscous forces.

**Q: What is thermodynamic efficiency?**
A: Thermodynamic efficiency is the ratio of useful work output to total energy input. The Carnot efficiency (1 − T_cold/T_hot) represents the maximum theoretical efficiency of a heat engine.

**Q: What is Bernoulli's principle in fluid dynamics?**
A: Bernoulli's principle states that in a flowing fluid, increased flow speed decreases pressure. It describes the conservation of energy in fluid flow and explains lift generation on airfoils.

**Q: What is PID control?**
A: PID (Proportional-Integral-Derivative) control is a feedback control algorithm that calculates a correction based on the error (P), accumulated error over time (I), and rate of error change (D) to maintain a setpoint.

**Q: What is stress and strain in materials?**
A: Stress is force per unit area (σ = F/A). Strain is the fractional change in dimension (ε = ΔL/L). Young's modulus E = σ/ε describes the stiffness of a material within its elastic limit.

**Q: What is Kirchhoff's circuit laws?**
A: Kirchhoff's current law: the sum of currents entering a node equals the sum leaving (conservation of charge). Kirchhoff's voltage law: the sum of voltages around any closed loop equals zero (conservation of energy).

**Q: What is signal sampling and Nyquist theorem?**
A: The Nyquist theorem states that to accurately reconstruct a signal, the sampling rate must be at least twice the highest frequency component. Undersampling causes aliasing (false frequencies).

**Q: What is failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA)?**
A: FMEA is a systematic method to identify potential failure modes in a system, assess their likelihood and severity, and implement mitigation measures. It is widely used in reliability engineering.

**Q: What is the difference between analog and digital signals?**
A: Analog signals are continuous in both time and amplitude. Digital signals are discrete, using binary (0/1) representation. Digital systems offer noise resistance, easier processing, and reliable storage.

**Q: What is Fourier analysis in engineering?**
A: Fourier analysis decomposes complex signals into constituent sinusoids, enabling frequency-domain analysis. The FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) is an efficient algorithm widely used in signal processing.

**Q: What is the concept of impedance in AC circuits?**
A: Impedance Z is the complex resistance to alternating current, combining resistance R and reactance X: Z = R + jX. Capacitors and inductors contribute frequency-dependent reactance.

**Q: What is a feedback loop in control systems?**
A: A feedback loop measures system output and feeds it back to the input to correct errors. Negative feedback stabilizes systems; positive feedback amplifies changes. PID controllers implement feedback control.

**Q: What is the principle of least action in mechanics?**
A: The principle of least action states that physical systems evolve along paths that minimize (or extremize) the action integral. It provides a foundation for Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics.

**Q: What is finite element analysis (FEA)?**
A: FEA is a numerical technique for solving differential equations by dividing complex structures into small elements. It is widely used to simulate mechanical stress, heat transfer, and fluid flow.

**Q: What is semiconductor doping in electronics?**
A: Doping adds impurities to semiconductors to control electrical properties. N-type doping (with phosphorus) adds electrons as majority carriers. P-type doping (with boron) creates electron holes.

**Q: What is the concept of entropy in information theory?**
A: Shannon entropy measures the average information content of a message source: H = −Σ p(x) log₂ p(x). It quantifies uncertainty and is fundamental to data compression and channel capacity.

**Q: What is the Rankine cycle?**
A: The Rankine cycle is the thermodynamic cycle used in steam power plants: water is heated to steam (boiler), expanded through a turbine (work output), condensed back to liquid, and pumped back to the boiler.

**Q: What is the concept of bandwidth in communications?**
A: Bandwidth is the range of frequencies a channel can transmit, or the maximum data rate a channel supports. Shannon's theorem: C = B log₂(1 + S/N) gives the theoretical maximum channel capacity.
