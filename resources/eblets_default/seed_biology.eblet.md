## Facts

**Q: What is DNA and what is its function?**
A: DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a double-helix molecule that carries genetic information in all living organisms, composed of nucleotide base pairs: adenine-thymine and guanine-cytosine.

**Q: What is the central dogma of molecular biology?**
A: The central dogma states that genetic information flows from DNA to RNA (transcription) to protein (translation). DNA encodes the instructions; proteins carry them out.

**Q: What is natural selection?**
A: Natural selection is the process by which heritable traits that improve survival and reproduction become more common in a population over generations. It is the primary mechanism of evolution.

**Q: What is cell division (mitosis vs meiosis)?**
A: Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells for growth and repair. Meiosis produces four genetically diverse haploid cells for sexual reproduction.

**Q: What is photosynthesis?**
A: Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy, CO₂, and water into glucose and oxygen: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

**Q: What is cellular respiration?**
A: Cellular respiration breaks down glucose to produce ATP energy: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP. It occurs in the mitochondria through glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain.

**Q: What is the structure and function of proteins?**
A: Proteins are polymers of amino acids that fold into specific 3D shapes. They function as enzymes, structural components, signaling molecules, antibodies, and transport proteins.

**Q: What is the immune system?**
A: The immune system defends the body against pathogens. The innate immune system provides rapid non-specific defense; the adaptive immune system generates specific antibodies and memory cells for long-term immunity.

**Q: What is a cell membrane and what does it do?**
A: The cell membrane (plasma membrane) is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins that controls what enters and exits the cell, maintains cellular environment, and mediates cell communication.

**Q: What are the differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?**
A: Prokaryotic cells (bacteria and archaea) lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Eukaryotic cells (animals, plants, fungi, protists) have a nucleus and complex organelles.

**Q: What is gene expression regulation?**
A: Gene expression is regulated at multiple levels: transcription factors bind promoters to activate/repress transcription; post-transcriptional regulation involves RNA splicing, stability; translation regulation controls protein synthesis.

**Q: What is the CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing system?**
A: CRISPR-Cas9 is a genome editing tool that uses guide RNA to direct the Cas9 nuclease to a specific DNA sequence, where it cuts both strands. This enables precise gene insertion, deletion, or modification.

**Q: What is the endocrine system?**
A: The endocrine system consists of glands that produce hormones released into the bloodstream to regulate metabolism, growth, development, reproduction, and homeostasis.

**Q: What is mendelian genetics?**
A: Mendel's laws describe trait inheritance: the law of segregation (allele pairs separate during meiosis) and the law of independent assortment (genes on different chromosomes sort independently).

**Q: What is the nervous system?**
A: The nervous system processes sensory information and coordinates responses. The central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) integrates information; the peripheral nervous system connects it to the body.

**Q: What is evolution by natural selection?**
A: Evolution is the change in heritable characteristics of biological populations over successive generations. Natural selection, genetic drift, mutation, and gene flow are the main mechanisms of evolutionary change.

**Q: What is the structure and function of the mitochondria?**
A: Mitochondria are organelles that produce ATP via oxidative phosphorylation. They have a double membrane; the inner membrane folds (cristae) house the electron transport chain. They have their own DNA.

**Q: What is homeostasis?**
A: Homeostasis is the ability of living organisms to maintain a stable internal environment despite external changes, through feedback mechanisms regulating temperature, pH, blood glucose, and other variables.

**Q: What is the cell cycle?**
A: The cell cycle is the series of events by which a cell grows and divides: G1 phase (growth), S phase (DNA synthesis), G2 phase (preparation), and M phase (mitosis). Checkpoints ensure fidelity.

**Q: What is an enzyme and how does it work?**
A: An enzyme is a biological catalyst (usually a protein) that speeds up chemical reactions by lowering activation energy. Each enzyme has an active site that specifically binds its substrate.
