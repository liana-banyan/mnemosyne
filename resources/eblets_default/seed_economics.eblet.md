## Facts

**Q: What is supply and demand?**
A: Supply and demand is the economic model where price adjusts to balance the quantity producers supply with the quantity consumers demand. Higher prices reduce demand and increase supply until equilibrium is reached.

**Q: What is GDP?**
A: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) measures the total monetary value of all goods and services produced within a country in a given period. It is the primary indicator of economic size and growth.

**Q: What is inflation?**
A: Inflation is the general increase in prices over time, decreasing purchasing power. It is measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Central banks target low, stable inflation (typically ~2%) to support economic stability.

**Q: What is comparative advantage in international trade?**
A: Comparative advantage states that countries should specialize in producing goods where they have the lowest opportunity cost and trade for others. This maximizes total production even if one country is absolutely more efficient at everything.

**Q: What is monetary policy?**
A: Monetary policy is a central bank's management of money supply and interest rates to achieve macroeconomic goals. Expansionary policy (lower rates, more money) stimulates growth; contractionary policy reduces inflation.

**Q: What is the concept of opportunity cost?**
A: Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative forgone when making a choice. Every economic decision involves a trade-off; rational decision-making requires weighing benefits against opportunity costs.

**Q: What is a market failure?**
A: Market failures occur when markets fail to allocate resources efficiently. Types include externalities (pollution), public goods (underprovided because non-excludable), information asymmetry, and monopoly power.

**Q: What is fiscal policy?**
A: Fiscal policy refers to government use of taxation and spending to influence the economy. Expansionary fiscal policy increases spending or cuts taxes to stimulate growth. Contractionary policy reduces deficit spending.

**Q: What is the Gini coefficient?**
A: The Gini coefficient measures income inequality within a population, ranging from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person has all income). It is widely used to compare inequality across countries and time.

**Q: What is game theory?**
A: Game theory analyzes strategic decision-making when outcomes depend on choices of multiple actors. The Nash equilibrium is a stable state where no player benefits from unilaterally changing their strategy.

**Q: What is the Phillips curve?**
A: The Phillips curve described an empirical inverse relationship between unemployment and inflation: lower unemployment was associated with higher inflation. The relationship became unstable in the 1970s stagflation era.

**Q: What is the concept of price elasticity?**
A: Price elasticity of demand measures how sensitive quantity demanded is to price changes. Elastic goods (|e| > 1) see proportionally larger quantity changes; inelastic goods (|e| < 1) show smaller quantity changes.

**Q: What is behavioral economics?**
A: Behavioral economics incorporates psychological insights into economic models, showing that people often deviate from rational choice due to biases (loss aversion, anchoring, present bias) and heuristics.

**Q: What are the key properties of public goods?**
A: Public goods are non-excludable (cannot prevent non-payers from using) and non-rival (one person's use doesn't reduce availability to others). Examples: national defense, lighthouses, basic research.

**Q: What is the concept of moral hazard in economics?**
A: Moral hazard occurs when one party takes more risks because they don't bear full consequences, often because another party absorbs them. Examples: insurance encouraging riskier behavior, banks taking excessive risk with government backing.

**Q: What is the efficient market hypothesis?**
A: The efficient market hypothesis (EMH) states that asset prices reflect all available information, making it impossible to consistently beat the market through analysis. Weak, semi-strong, and strong forms differ in information included.

**Q: What is the Keynesian theory of aggregate demand?**
A: Keynesian economics holds that aggregate demand (consumption + investment + government spending + net exports) drives economic output. In recessions, government spending can stimulate demand and restore full employment.

**Q: What is externalities and the Coase theorem?**
A: Externalities are costs or benefits affecting third parties not involved in a transaction. The Coase theorem states that if property rights are well-defined and transaction costs are low, private parties can solve externality problems.

**Q: What is the concept of diminishing marginal returns?**
A: Diminishing marginal returns states that adding more of one input while holding others fixed eventually yields progressively smaller increases in output. A classic example: additional workers in a factory of fixed size.

**Q: What is the role of interest rates in the economy?**
A: Interest rates, set by central banks, are the price of borrowing money. Lower rates stimulate borrowing and investment (expansionary); higher rates reduce inflation by cooling spending and investment (contractionary).
