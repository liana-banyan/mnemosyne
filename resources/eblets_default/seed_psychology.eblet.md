## Facts

**Q: What is classical conditioning?**
A: Classical conditioning (Pavlov) is learning by association: a neutral stimulus paired with an unconditioned stimulus comes to elicit the same response. Pavlov's dogs learned to salivate at a bell associated with food.

**Q: What is operant conditioning?**
A: Operant conditioning (Skinner) uses consequences to modify behavior: reinforcement (positive or negative) increases behavior; punishment (positive or negative) decreases behavior. Schedules of reinforcement affect learning rates.

**Q: What are the major personality models in psychology?**
A: The Big Five (OCEAN) model describes personality along five dimensions: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism. It is the most empirically supported framework in personality psychology.

**Q: What is cognitive dissonance?**
A: Cognitive dissonance is the mental discomfort experienced when holding contradictory beliefs or when behavior conflicts with beliefs. People reduce dissonance by changing beliefs, changing behavior, or rationalizing the conflict.

**Q: What is the concept of confirmation bias?**
A: Confirmation bias is the tendency to search for, interpret, and recall information in ways that confirm one's preexisting beliefs. It is one of the most widely documented cognitive biases.

**Q: What are Maslow's hierarchy of needs?**
A: Maslow's hierarchy arranges human needs in order: physiological (food, shelter) → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualization. Lower needs must be met before higher ones become motivating.

**Q: What is the Stanford Prison Experiment and its implications?**
A: Zimbardo's 1971 Stanford Prison Experiment assigned participants to guard/prisoner roles and was stopped early due to abuse. It highlighted how situational factors can profoundly influence behavior, though its methodology has been questioned.

**Q: What is the concept of attachment theory?**
A: Bowlby's attachment theory holds that children have an innate need to form close emotional bonds with caregivers. Ainsworth identified secure, anxious, and avoidant attachment styles that influence later relationships.

**Q: What is social learning theory?**
A: Bandura's social learning theory states that people learn by observing others (modeling). His Bobo doll experiment showed children imitated aggressive behavior. Behavior is influenced by observation, imitation, and reinforcement.

**Q: What is the bystander effect?**
A: The bystander effect states that individuals are less likely to help in an emergency when others are present. Diffusion of responsibility and pluralistic ignorance explain why larger groups respond less to victims.

**Q: What is working memory?**
A: Working memory is the cognitive system that temporarily holds and manipulates information. Baddeley's model includes the phonological loop (verbal), visuospatial sketchpad (visual), and central executive (attention control).

**Q: What is the difference between explicit and implicit memory?**
A: Explicit (declarative) memory involves conscious recall of facts (semantic) and events (episodic). Implicit memory operates without conscious awareness, including procedural skills, priming, and conditioning.

**Q: What is neuroplasticity?**
A: Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to change its structure and function through experience. Synaptic connections strengthen with use (long-term potentiation) and weaken without it (synaptic pruning).

**Q: What is the fundamental attribution error?**
A: The fundamental attribution error is the tendency to over-attribute others' behavior to their character (dispositional factors) while underestimating situational influences, while doing the opposite for ourselves.

**Q: What is the concept of schemas in cognitive psychology?**
A: Schemas are mental frameworks that organize and interpret information based on prior knowledge. They speed up processing but can lead to errors by filling in missing information with expected (not actual) content.

**Q: What is the DSM and how does it classify mental disorders?**
A: The DSM (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual) is the standard American psychiatric classification system. It categorizes mental disorders by symptom criteria, enabling consistent diagnosis and research.

**Q: What is the Stroop effect?**
A: The Stroop effect demonstrates cognitive interference: naming the ink color of color words is slower when the word and ink color conflict (e.g., "RED" written in blue ink). It reveals automaticity of reading.

**Q: What is the concept of resilience in psychology?**
A: Psychological resilience is the capacity to recover from adversity, trauma, or stress. It involves positive adaptation despite challenges and is influenced by individual factors, social support, and coping strategies.

**Q: What is the halo effect?**
A: The halo effect is the cognitive bias where a positive impression of one trait (e.g., physical attractiveness) influences perception of other unrelated traits (e.g., intelligence, competence).

**Q: What is the Milgram experiment?**
A: Milgram's obedience experiments showed that most participants would administer apparently dangerous electric shocks to strangers when ordered to do so by an authority figure, demonstrating the power of authority on behavior.
