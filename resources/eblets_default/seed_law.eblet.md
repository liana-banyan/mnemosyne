## Facts

**Q: What is the difference between civil law and criminal law?**
A: Criminal law involves offenses against the state and society; the government prosecutes and punishment includes imprisonment. Civil law involves disputes between individuals; remedies are typically monetary damages or injunctions.

**Q: What is the concept of due process?**
A: Due process requires the government to respect all legal rights owed to a person under the law. Procedural due process ensures fair procedures; substantive due process protects certain fundamental rights from government interference.

**Q: What is tort law?**
A: Tort law covers civil wrongs (other than breach of contract) that cause harm. Key torts include negligence (duty of care + breach + causation + damages), intentional torts (assault, battery), and strict liability.

**Q: What is contract law?**
A: A valid contract requires offer, acceptance, consideration (something of value exchanged), and legal capacity. Breach of contract allows the non-breaching party to seek damages or specific performance.

**Q: What is the rule of law?**
A: The rule of law is the principle that all individuals, institutions, and the government itself are accountable to laws that are publicly promulgated, equally enforced, and independently adjudicated.

**Q: What is habeas corpus?**
A: Habeas corpus is a legal writ requiring authorities to bring a detained person before a court to determine whether their detention is lawful. It is a fundamental protection against unlawful imprisonment.

**Q: What is the doctrine of precedent (stare decisis)?**
A: Stare decisis is the legal principle that courts should follow prior decisions (precedents) in similar cases. This promotes consistency, predictability, and stability in the legal system.

**Q: What is intellectual property law?**
A: Intellectual property law protects creations of the mind. Patents protect inventions (20 years). Copyrights protect creative works (life + 70 years). Trademarks protect brand identifiers.

**Q: What is the Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable searches?**
A: The Fourth Amendment prohibits unreasonable searches and seizures and requires warrants based on probable cause. Evidence obtained through illegal searches may be excluded (exclusionary rule).

**Q: What is corporate law?**
A: Corporate law governs the formation, operation, and dissolution of corporations. Key concepts include limited liability (shareholders only risk investment), fiduciary duties of directors, and corporate governance structures.

**Q: What is international humanitarian law?**
A: International humanitarian law (the laws of war) regulates armed conflict, protecting civilians and combatants. Key instruments include the Geneva Conventions. It establishes war crimes and crimes against humanity.

**Q: What is the principle of freedom of speech in US constitutional law?**
A: The First Amendment protects freedom of speech from government restriction. However, it is not absolute - exceptions include incitement to imminent lawless action, true threats, and obscenity.

**Q: What is administrative law?**
A: Administrative law governs the activities of governmental agencies, including rulemaking, adjudication, and enforcement. Judicial review can overturn agency actions that are arbitrary, capricious, or unconstitutional.

**Q: What is the concept of mens rea in criminal law?**
A: Mens rea is the mental element of a crime - the guilty mind. Most crimes require both a guilty act (actus reus) and guilty mind. Levels include intent, knowledge, recklessness, and negligence.

**Q: What is environmental law?**
A: Environmental law regulates human interaction with the environment. In the US, laws include the Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, and NEPA. International agreements include the Paris Climate Agreement.

**Q: What is the concept of standing to sue?**
A: Standing requires a plaintiff to have a concrete, particularized injury fairly traceable to the defendant that would be redressed by a favorable ruling. Without standing, courts lack jurisdiction.

**Q: What is comparative fault in tort law?**
A: Comparative fault allocates damages based on each party's percentage of fault. Under pure comparative fault, a plaintiff can recover even if 99% at fault. Modified comparative fault bars recovery above 50% fault.

**Q: What is the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)?**
A: The UCC is a set of standardized laws governing commercial transactions in the United States, including sales of goods, negotiable instruments, and secured transactions. Most states have adopted it.

**Q: What is a class action lawsuit?**
A: A class action allows many people with similar claims to sue as a group (class). It is efficient for small individual claims (like consumer fraud) that would be impractical to litigate individually.

**Q: What is the exclusionary rule?**
A: The exclusionary rule prevents illegally obtained evidence from being used in criminal trials. It deters police misconduct by removing the incentive to conduct illegal searches. Exceptions include good faith reliance.
