## Facts

**Q: What is Newton's first law of motion?**
A: Newton's first law states that an object at rest remains at rest and an object in motion remains in motion at constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force.

**Q: What is Newton's second law of motion?**
A: Newton's second law states F = ma: the net force on an object equals its mass times acceleration. Force and acceleration are in the same direction.

**Q: What is Newton's third law of motion?**
A: Newton's third law states that for every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force. Forces always occur in pairs between interacting objects.

**Q: What is the law of conservation of energy?**
A: The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted between forms. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

**Q: What is the speed of light in a vacuum?**
A: The speed of light in a vacuum is approximately 3 × 10⁸ meters per second (299,792,458 m/s). It is a fundamental constant denoted c.

**Q: What is Ohm's law?**
A: Ohm's law states V = IR: voltage (V) equals current (I) times resistance (R). It describes the relationship between electrical quantities in many conductors.

**Q: What is the photoelectric effect?**
A: The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a material when light of sufficient frequency shines on it. Einstein explained it by proposing that light consists of discrete packets (photons) of energy E = hf.

**Q: What is the Heisenberg uncertainty principle?**
A: The Heisenberg uncertainty principle states that the position and momentum of a particle cannot both be precisely known simultaneously: Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2, where ℏ is the reduced Planck constant.

**Q: What is the law of conservation of momentum?**
A: The law of conservation of momentum states that the total momentum of an isolated system remains constant. When objects interact, momentum is transferred but the total sum is preserved.

**Q: What is the Doppler effect?**
A: The Doppler effect is the change in frequency of a wave (sound or light) as the source and observer move relative to each other. Approaching sources produce higher frequencies; receding sources produce lower frequencies.

**Q: What is entropy in thermodynamics?**
A: Entropy is a measure of the disorder or randomness in a system. The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system always increases over time, never decreases.

**Q: What is the electromagnetic spectrum?**
A: The electromagnetic spectrum encompasses all types of electromagnetic radiation ordered by frequency and wavelength: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.

**Q: What is quantum entanglement?**
A: Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where two particles become correlated such that measuring one particle instantly affects the other, regardless of distance. This is a fundamental feature of quantum mechanics.

**Q: What is special relativity's mass-energy equivalence?**
A: Einstein's mass-energy equivalence E = mc² states that mass and energy are interconvertible, with c being the speed of light. A small amount of mass converts to an enormous amount of energy.

**Q: What is a black hole?**
A: A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape once it passes the event horizon. Black holes form from collapsed massive stars.

**Q: What is the Bohr model of the atom?**
A: The Bohr model describes electrons orbiting the nucleus in discrete energy levels (shells). Electrons emit or absorb photons when transitioning between levels, explaining atomic emission spectra.

**Q: What is the difference between conduction, convection, and radiation as heat transfer mechanisms?**
A: Conduction transfers heat through direct contact between materials. Convection transfers heat through fluid movement (currents). Radiation transfers heat through electromagnetic waves, requiring no medium.

**Q: What is Coulomb's law?**
A: Coulomb's law states that the electrostatic force between two point charges is F = k·q₁q₂/r², proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.

**Q: What is the concept of wave-particle duality?**
A: Wave-particle duality is the quantum mechanical principle that every particle also has wave-like properties, and every wave also has particle-like properties, as demonstrated by double-slit experiments.

**Q: What is nuclear fission?**
A: Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy atomic nucleus (like uranium-235 or plutonium-239) into lighter nuclei, releasing large amounts of energy. It powers nuclear reactors and atomic bombs.
