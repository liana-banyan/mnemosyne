## Facts

**Q: What are the main risk factors for cardiovascular disease?**
A: Major cardiovascular risk factors include hypertension, high LDL cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, family history, and age. Lifestyle modifications and medications significantly reduce risk.

**Q: What is the germ theory of disease?**
A: Germ theory (19th century, Pasteur, Koch) established that specific microorganisms cause specific diseases. It replaced miasma theory and revolutionized medicine, leading to vaccines, antibiotics, and sterile surgical technique.

**Q: What is the difference between viruses and bacteria?**
A: Bacteria are single-celled organisms that can reproduce independently. Viruses are non-living particles that require host cells to replicate. Antibiotics treat bacterial infections but are ineffective against viruses.

**Q: What is evidence-based medicine?**
A: Evidence-based medicine (EBM) integrates best available research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values to guide healthcare decisions. The hierarchy of evidence ranks randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses highest.

**Q: What is herd immunity?**
A: Herd immunity occurs when a sufficient proportion of a population has immunity (through vaccination or prior infection) to prevent disease spread, protecting those who cannot be vaccinated. The threshold varies by disease transmissibility.

**Q: What is the human body's immune response to infection?**
A: The innate immune response is rapid and non-specific (inflammation, fever, natural killer cells). The adaptive immune response is specific: B cells produce antibodies; T cells directly kill infected cells and coordinate responses.

**Q: What is the significance of blood pressure readings?**
A: Blood pressure is recorded as systolic (heart contracting) / diastolic (heart relaxing) in mmHg. Normal is below 120/80. Hypertension (≥130/80) significantly increases risk of stroke, heart attack, and kidney disease.

**Q: What are the principles of clinical trials?**
A: Clinical trials evaluate medical interventions in humans. Phases I (safety), II (efficacy), III (comparison vs. standard treatment), and IV (post-market surveillance) ensure evidence before approval. Randomization and blinding minimize bias.

**Q: What is the difference between incidence and prevalence in epidemiology?**
A: Incidence is the rate of new cases of a disease in a population during a defined period. Prevalence is the proportion of a population with a disease at a given time. Prevalence = incidence × average disease duration.

**Q: What is antibiotic resistance?**
A: Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria evolve mechanisms to survive antibiotic treatment. Overuse and misuse of antibiotics accelerate resistance. It is a major global health threat, causing millions of deaths annually.

**Q: What is the role of public health?**
A: Public health focuses on protecting and improving community health through surveillance, prevention, and policy. Key functions: disease surveillance, vaccination programs, sanitation, health education, and emergency response.

**Q: What is the body mass index (BMI) and its limitations?**
A: BMI = weight(kg) / height(m)². Normal is 18.5-24.9; overweight 25-29.9; obese ≥30. Limitations: doesn't distinguish fat from muscle, doesn't account for fat distribution, not accurate for all ethnic groups.

**Q: What are the stages of grief according to Kübler-Ross?**
A: Kübler-Ross described five stages of grief: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance. These are not linear and people experience them differently. The model has evolved to recognize grief as more complex and individual.

**Q: What is the significance of the microbiome?**
A: The human microbiome consists of trillions of microorganisms in and on the body, especially the gut. The gut microbiome influences digestion, immune function, mental health, and disease susceptibility.

**Q: What is metabolic syndrome?**
A: Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of conditions (central obesity, high blood pressure, high blood sugar, abnormal cholesterol) occurring together. It significantly increases risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

**Q: What is the difference between type 1 and type 2 diabetes?**
A: Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune disease destroying insulin-producing cells, requiring lifelong insulin. Type 2 diabetes is characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency, often linked to lifestyle factors.

**Q: What is a randomized controlled trial (RCT)?**
A: An RCT randomly assigns participants to intervention or control groups to measure treatment effects. Randomization prevents selection bias; blinding prevents performance and detection bias. RCTs are the gold standard for causal inference.

**Q: What is mental health and the biopsychosocial model?**
A: Mental health encompasses emotional, psychological, and social well-being. The biopsychosocial model explains mental illness as resulting from biological (genetics, neurobiology), psychological (trauma, thoughts), and social (support, environment) factors.

**Q: What are vaccines and how do they work?**
A: Vaccines stimulate the immune system to recognize and fight specific pathogens without causing disease. They present antigens (killed/attenuated pathogens, subunits, or mRNA) to train B and T cells to mount a rapid response.

**Q: What is the opioid epidemic?**
A: The opioid epidemic in North America began with overprescription of opioid pain medications in the 1990s, leading to widespread addiction, transition to heroin and fentanyl, and over 500,000 overdose deaths in the US 1999-2019.
