## Facts

**Q: What is the periodic table?**
A: The periodic table organizes elements by increasing atomic number, with elements in the same column (group) sharing similar chemical properties due to having the same number of valence electrons.

**Q: What is a covalent bond?**
A: A covalent bond forms when two atoms share one or more pairs of electrons, creating a stable molecule. Examples include H₂, O₂, H₂O, and CO₂.

**Q: What is an ionic bond?**
A: An ionic bond forms when one atom transfers electrons to another, creating oppositely charged ions (cations and anions) that attract each other electrostatically. Example: NaCl (table salt).

**Q: What is Avogadro's number?**
A: Avogadro's number is approximately 6.022 × 10²³ and represents the number of particles (atoms, molecules, or ions) in one mole of a substance.

**Q: What is the ideal gas law?**
A: The ideal gas law PV = nRT relates pressure (P), volume (V), number of moles (n), the ideal gas constant (R = 8.314 J/mol·K), and temperature (T) in Kelvin.

**Q: What is oxidation-reduction (redox)?**
A: Oxidation is the loss of electrons and reduction is the gain of electrons. These always occur together in redox reactions. The species oxidized loses electrons to the species being reduced.

**Q: What is pH and how is it measured?**
A: pH measures the acidity or basicity of a solution: pH = −log[H⁺]. pH < 7 is acidic, pH = 7 is neutral, pH > 7 is basic. Buffers resist changes in pH.

**Q: What is the law of conservation of mass?**
A: The law of conservation of mass states that in a chemical reaction, the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products. Matter is neither created nor destroyed.

**Q: What are isotopes?**
A: Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons, giving them different mass numbers while retaining the same chemical properties.

**Q: What is a catalyst?**
A: A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed. It works by providing an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy.

**Q: What is electronegativity?**
A: Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract electrons toward itself in a chemical bond. Fluorine has the highest electronegativity; it decreases down groups and increases across periods.

**Q: What is Le Chatelier's principle?**
A: Le Chatelier's principle states that when a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to counteract the disturbance and restore equilibrium. Changes in concentration, pressure, or temperature shift equilibria.

**Q: What is a mole in chemistry?**
A: A mole is the SI unit for amount of substance, equal to 6.022 × 10²³ entities. One mole of any element contains Avogadro's number of atoms; the molar mass in grams equals the atomic mass in amu.

**Q: What is the structure of DNA at the chemical level?**
A: DNA consists of two antiparallel nucleotide chains linked by hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs (adenine-thymine, guanine-cytosine), forming a double helix.

**Q: What is entropy in chemistry?**
A: Entropy (S) measures the disorder of a system. Gibbs free energy ΔG = ΔH − TΔS determines whether a reaction is spontaneous (ΔG < 0). Systems spontaneously evolve toward higher entropy.

**Q: What is activation energy?**
A: Activation energy is the minimum energy required for a chemical reaction to occur. The Arrhenius equation describes how reaction rate depends exponentially on activation energy and temperature.

**Q: What are the properties of acids and bases in the Brønsted-Lowry theory?**
A: In Brønsted-Lowry theory, an acid is a proton (H⁺) donor and a base is a proton acceptor. Every acid-base reaction involves conjugate acid-base pairs.

**Q: What is a polymer?**
A: A polymer is a large molecule made of many repeated smaller units (monomers) linked by covalent bonds. Examples include polyethylene, nylon, proteins, and DNA.

**Q: What is chromatography?**
A: Chromatography is a separation technique based on differential migration of components through a stationary phase carried by a mobile phase. Types include gas chromatography (GC) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).

**Q: What is Hess's law?**
A: Hess's law states that the total enthalpy change for a reaction is the same regardless of the number of steps, because enthalpy is a state function. This allows calculation of unmeasurable enthalpy changes.
