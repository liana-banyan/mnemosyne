## Facts

**Q: What was the Magna Carta?**
A: The Magna Carta, signed in 1215 by King John of England, established that the king was subject to the rule of law and granted certain rights to barons, laying groundwork for constitutional governance.

**Q: What caused World War I?**
A: WWI (1914-1918) was triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria in 1914. Underlying causes included militarism, alliance systems, imperialism, and rising nationalism.

**Q: What was the French Revolution?**
A: The French Revolution (1789-1799) overthrew the French monarchy, established a republic, and ended with Napoleon's rise. Its ideals of liberty, equality, and fraternity influenced democratic movements worldwide.

**Q: What was the Cold War?**
A: The Cold War (1947-1991) was the geopolitical tension between the USA and Soviet Union after WWII. It featured an arms race, space race, proxy wars, and ideological competition between capitalism and communism.

**Q: What was the Renaissance?**
A: The Renaissance (14th-17th centuries) was a European cultural movement that revived classical Greek and Roman knowledge. It produced advances in art (Leonardo, Michelangelo), science (Galileo), and literature (Shakespeare).

**Q: What were the causes and effects of the Industrial Revolution?**
A: The Industrial Revolution (18th-19th century, starting in Britain) transformed economies from agricultural to industrial through mechanization. It created urbanization, new labor patterns, and eventually modern standards of living.

**Q: What was the fall of the Roman Empire?**
A: The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 CE due to multiple causes: military pressures from Germanic tribes, economic problems, political instability, and overextension. The Eastern (Byzantine) Empire continued until 1453.

**Q: What was the Silk Road?**
A: The Silk Road was a network of trade routes connecting East Asia to the Mediterranean from roughly 200 BCE to 1450 CE. It facilitated exchange of silk, spices, technologies, ideas, and diseases across civilizations.

**Q: What was the Holocaust?**
A: The Holocaust was the systematic genocide of approximately six million Jewish people and millions of others by Nazi Germany and its collaborators from 1941-1945, marking the worst genocide in modern history.

**Q: What was the American Civil War?**
A: The American Civil War (1861-1865) was fought between the Union (North) and Confederacy (South). Primary cause: the institution of slavery. The Union victory preserved the nation and led to the abolition of slavery.

**Q: What was the Scientific Revolution?**
A: The Scientific Revolution (16th-17th centuries) transformed understanding of nature through empirical observation and mathematical description. Key figures: Copernicus (heliocentrism), Galileo (experiment), Newton (laws of motion).

**Q: What was colonialism and its legacy?**
A: Colonialism was the practice of establishing political control over other territories, practiced extensively by European powers from the 15th-20th centuries. Its legacy includes economic inequality, cultural disruption, and borders that cause modern conflicts.

**Q: What was the Enlightenment?**
A: The Enlightenment (18th century) was an intellectual movement emphasizing reason, science, and individual rights over tradition and religious authority. It influenced the American and French Revolutions and modern democracy.

**Q: What was the printing press and why was it significant?**
A: The printing press invented by Gutenberg (~1440) enabled mass production of books, spreading literacy and knowledge across Europe. It facilitated the Reformation, Scientific Revolution, and ultimately democratization of information.

**Q: What was the decolonization movement of the 20th century?**
A: Decolonization was the process by which colonies in Asia, Africa, and the Americas gained independence from European powers after WWII. Between 1945-1975, dozens of new nations emerged from former colonial territories.

**Q: What was the impact of the Black Death?**
A: The Black Death pandemic (1347-1351) killed 30-60% of Europe's population. Its aftermath contributed to the decline of feudalism, rising wages for surviving workers, and social changes that shaped the Renaissance.

**Q: What was the Russian Revolution?**
A: The Russian Revolution (1917) comprised two events: the February Revolution (which ended Tsarist rule) and the October Revolution (in which the Bolsheviks under Lenin seized power), establishing the Soviet Union.

**Q: What was the significance of the Moon landing in 1969?**
A: The Apollo 11 mission on July 20, 1969 landed Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the Moon, with Armstrong becoming the first human to walk on the Moon. It was the culmination of the Space Race between the USA and USSR.

**Q: What were the Crusades?**
A: The Crusades (1096-1291) were a series of religious wars sanctioned by the Catholic Church, primarily aimed at retaking the Holy Land from Muslim control. They shaped medieval Europe-Middle East relations and trade.

**Q: What was apartheid in South Africa?**
A: Apartheid (1948-1994) was a system of institutionalized racial segregation in South Africa. Nelson Mandela led the ANC resistance; international sanctions and internal pressure ended the system, and Mandela became president in 1994.
